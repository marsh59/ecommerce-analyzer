🎯 RFM Analysis

What is RFM Analysis?

RFM Analysis is a customer segmentation technique used in marketing and business intelligence. It ranks every customer on three dimensions: Recency — how recently they made a purchase (fewer days = better), Frequency — how many times they ordered (more orders = better), and Monetary — how much total money they spent (higher amount = better). Each customer receives a score from 1 to 4 on each dimension, and based on the combination of these scores, they are placed into a segment. This helps the business understand exactly which customers are thriving, which need attention, and which may already be lost — so resources can be directed where they will have the most impact.

Customer Segments Overview

🏆 Champions

Definition: Champions are your absolute best customers. They bought very recently, buy very often, and spend the most money. These are the customers who love your business and come back repeatedly without needing to be reminded. They score 4 out of 4 on all three RFM dimensions.
Insight: You have {{ total_champions }} Champion customers, each spending an average of £{{ champ_avg }}. These customers are irreplaceable — they drive a disproportionate share of your revenue. Reward them with exclusive early access, loyalty discounts, or personalised thank-you messages to strengthen their attachment and prevent them from switching to competitors.
💙 Loyal
{{ total_loyal }}
Definition: Loyal customers buy regularly and spend reasonably well, but may not have purchased as recently or as frequently as Champions. They are consistent, dependable buyers who have a genuine relationship with the business. They score 3 or above on both Recency and Frequency.
Insight: You have {{ total_loyal }} Loyal customers, averaging £{{ loyal_avg }} in spending. This is your largest high-value group. With the right engagement — such as product recommendations, seasonal offers, or referral incentives — many of these customers have the potential to become Champions. Keeping them engaged consistently is key to growing this segment.
⚠ At Risk
{{ total_at_risk }}
Definition: At Risk customers were once frequent buyers, but they have not purchased recently. Their buying behaviour has slowed down significantly, and if left unattended, they are likely to stop buying altogether. They score high on Frequency but low on Recency — meaning they used to buy often, but something has changed.
Insight: You have {{ total_at_risk }} At Risk customers, with an average spend of £{{ risk_avg }}. This group requires immediate attention. A targeted win-back campaign — such as a personalised discount, a "We miss you" email, or a reminder about new products they previously bought — could re-activate a significant portion of these customers before they become Lost. The cost of re-engaging an existing customer is far lower than acquiring a new one.
❌ Lost
{{ total_lost }}
Definition: Lost customers have not purchased in a very long time and also did not buy frequently when they were active. They score low on both Recency and Frequency, suggesting the relationship with the business has effectively ended.
Insight: You have {{ total_lost }} Lost customers. While some may be permanently gone, a small-scale re-engagement campaign with a compelling offer is worth attempting — even recovering a small fraction can be profitable. For the majority, resources are better spent on retaining Champions and converting At Risk customers rather than pursuing Lost ones aggressively.
🆕 New Customer
{{ total_new_customer }}
Definition: New Customers bought recently but have not yet established a frequent buying pattern. They scored high on Recency but low on Frequency, meaning they are still in the early stages of their relationship with the business.
Insight: You have {{ total_new_customer }} New Customers. These customers represent a major growth opportunity. A well-timed follow-up — such as a product recommendation email, an introductory discount on their next order, or a welcome series — can significantly increase the chance that they become Loyal or even Champion customers over time. First impressions matter, and nurturing new customers early pays long-term dividends.
